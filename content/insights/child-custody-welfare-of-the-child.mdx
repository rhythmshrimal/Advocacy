Of everything that is contested when a marriage breaks down, custody is the one issue where the court is not really deciding between two parties. It is deciding for a third person who is not litigating at all.

That is why the governing principle in Indian law is short and settled: **the welfare of the child is paramount.** Statutory provisions, parental rights, and even the parties' own agreement all yield to it.

## Custody is not the same as guardianship

The two are often used interchangeably, and they are not the same thing.

- **Custody** concerns who the child lives with day to day, and how the other parent's access is structured.
- **Guardianship** concerns the legal authority to take decisions for the child and to deal with the child's property.

A parent may have custody without being the natural guardian, and a guardian may not have custody. Proceedings are typically brought under the **Guardians and Wards Act, 1890**, read with the personal law applicable to the parties - for Hindus, the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956, which recognises that the custody of a child below the age of five ordinarily lies with the mother.

## What the court actually looks at

The welfare principle is not vague in application. Courts examine concrete things:

- The **age and needs of the child**, including health, schooling, and emotional stability.
- The **existing arrangement** and how long it has been in place - continuity matters, and courts are reluctant to uproot a settled child without good reason.
- The **capacity of each parent** to provide care, in time and attention as much as in money.
- The **child's own preference**, where the child is old enough to form an intelligent view. This is considered, not treated as decisive.
- The **conduct of each parent** so far as it bears on the child - including any attempt to alienate the child from the other parent.

> Financial superiority is not the test. A parent with a larger income does not, for that reason, have a stronger claim.

## Access is a right of the child

Except where contact would genuinely harm the child, courts treat the child's relationship with both parents as worth preserving. Orders commonly provide for structured visitation - weekends, holidays, video contact - and increasingly for shared parenting arrangements where the parents can cooperate enough to make them work.

Interim custody and interim visitation can be sought while the main petition is pending, which matters a great deal when a child has been removed or contact has been cut off.

## Custody orders are not final in the ordinary sense

Because the welfare of the child is a continuing consideration, a custody arrangement can be modified if circumstances change materially - a relocation, a change in the child's needs, or a breakdown in the existing arrangement. This cuts both ways: an unfavourable order is not necessarily permanent, and a favourable one is not immune from review.

## The takeaway

The strongest custody case is usually the one that reads least like a fight. Documented involvement in the child's routine, a workable proposal for the other parent's access, and a demonstrated willingness to keep the child out of the dispute all carry weight - because they are evidence of exactly what the court is looking for.
