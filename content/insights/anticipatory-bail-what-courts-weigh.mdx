Anticipatory bail is one of the few remedies in criminal law that works *before* the damage is done. Where a person has a reasonable apprehension of arrest in a non-bailable offence, the law allows them to approach the court in advance and seek a direction that, in the event of arrest, they shall be released on bail.

The provision most people know it by is **Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure**, now carried forward into the corresponding provision of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita. The section number has changed; the principle has not.

## It is a discretion, not an entitlement

The court is not simply asked to grant protection - it is asked to be satisfied that protection is warranted. In practice, the factors that recur across these applications are:

- The **nature and gravity** of the accusation, and the specific role attributed to the applicant.
- The applicant's **antecedents** - including whether they have previously been involved in a similar offence.
- Whether there is a real likelihood of the applicant **fleeing from justice**.
- Whether the accusation appears to have been made with the object of **injuring or humiliating** the applicant.
- The extent to which custodial interrogation is genuinely required, and the risk of **evidence being tampered with** or witnesses influenced.

> The Supreme Court has cautioned against reducing this discretion to a formula - it is neither to be exercised as a matter of routine nor withheld as a rarity.

## Timing and forum

Two practical points decide more anticipatory bail applications than any argument on merits.

**First, timing.** The remedy is available where there is an apprehension of arrest - so it must be invoked before arrest, not after. Once a person is taken into custody, the application to be made is one for regular bail. Moving early, on a properly drafted apprehension, is what keeps the option open at all.

**Second, forum.** The application lies before the Court of Session or the High Court. Which of the two to approach first is a strategic decision that turns on the offence, the stage of investigation, and the practice of the court concerned. Interim protection can often be sought pending final hearing.

## Conditions come with the order

Anticipatory bail is rarely unconditional. Courts routinely require that the applicant make themselves available for interrogation as and when required, that they not directly or indirectly influence any witness, and that they not leave the country without permission. These conditions are part of the protection, and breaching them is the fastest way to lose it.

On duration, a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court has clarified that an order granting anticipatory bail need not be limited to a fixed period and can, in appropriate cases, continue until the end of trial - though courts retain the power to impose limits on the facts of a given case.

## Where the remedy is not available

Some special statutes expressly exclude anticipatory bail, and others impose additional conditions that must be satisfied before any bail is granted. Whether the remedy is even open to you is therefore the first question to settle - before drafting begins.

## The takeaway

An anticipatory bail application is won on preparation: a candid account of the facts, a clear explanation of why custody serves no investigative purpose, and a record that answers the accusation rather than avoiding it. If you have reason to believe an FIR has been or is about to be registered against you, seek advice before you are arrested - not after.
