When both spouses agree to part ways, a **mutual-consent divorce** is usually the least painful and most dignified route. It avoids the adversarial machinery of a contested petition and lets the parties decide the terms themselves. But it still follows a defined process, and understanding that process helps set realistic expectations.

## The two-motion process

A mutual-consent divorce typically proceeds in two stages:

- **First motion:** Both parties jointly file the petition and record their statements before the court, confirming that they have agreed to dissolve the marriage and have settled matters such as maintenance, custody, and the return of property.
- **Second motion:** After a statutory interval, the parties return to confirm that their decision stands, and the court passes the decree of divorce.

Between the two motions lies the **cooling-off period** - a window intended to give the parties time to reflect.

## The cooling-off period - and when it can be waived

The cooling-off period is designed as a safeguard against impulsive decisions. However, courts have recognised that where:

- the parties have **already been separated** for a substantial time,
- there is **no realistic possibility** of reconciliation, and
- **all issues** between them are genuinely settled,

the waiting period can serve no purpose and may be **waived**. Whether a waiver is appropriate depends on the facts, and it is sought by application to the court.

> A well-documented settlement and a clear record of separation strengthen an application to waive the waiting period.

## Settling the terms well

The strength of a mutual-consent divorce lies in its settlement. Time spent getting the terms right - maintenance or a one-time settlement, custody and visitation, and the treatment of jointly held assets - is time well spent. A clear, complete settlement reduces the chance of disputes resurfacing later.

## The takeaway

Mutual consent is often the wisest path when a marriage has genuinely ended. With the terms properly settled and the record in order, it can be both dignified and reasonably time-bound.
